# A backlog rate needs a definition

This is a working rule for the vault, compiled from three library records. It is not a law of markets, and it is not investment advice. Conviction is 4 out of 5 because the concrete case is one fictional issuer plus a primer. Another issuer with the same pattern would raise it. A sourced case where a confident note was still comparable without those three disclosures would lower it.

`last_shown` is 2026-03-21, the day this page was accepted. A later recall did not change this page. See the review of 2 Apr 2026.

- [lib-primer-001](../../library/lib-primer-001.md), Comparability primer, posted 2 Nov 2025. The general rule.
- [lib-lom-003](../../library/lib-lom-003.md), Port Hinge Ledger, posted 11 Mar 2026. The same issuer prints a second total once options are removed.
- [lib-lom-004](../../library/lib-lom-004.md), synthetic Q&A, posted 18 Mar 2026. A different source confirms the narrower definition.

[lib-lom-005](../../library/lib-lom-005.md) repeats the ledger under a new link. It is a duplicate, so it is not a fourth supporting record.

## Evidence against

- [lib-lom-001](../../library/lib-lom-001.md), Lantern Desk Notes, posted 14 Jan 2026. A named desk published 22% growth on the wider total. That is the counterexample: the note sounded finished. The current belief is that the desk's confidence did not supply the missing split, which the March sources then supplied. The January claim stays on file and is marked superseded. Its sentence was not rewritten.

## Current belief

The January Low Oriole figure and the March figure are different objects. Repeating 22% after 11 Mar 2026 would mix them. The ledger already says the $68 million gap is unexercised options, so that composition is sourced. What this page adds is the rule for the next figure: stop when any of the three disclosures is missing.
